## Cooling path (one column)

1. Room air enters the outer face through the printed grille and a MERV 8 filter.
2. The **tracing coil** chills it. That is 3 rows of 3/8 in copper serpentine with 12 FPI aluminium fins, 32 passes per row at 25.4 mm tube pitch, and 4 water circuits (2 per 8-GPU group).
3. The chilled air crosses the cable zone and is drawn in by each card's blower through the 10.8 mm gap above its shroud (50.8 mm slot pitch).
4. The blower pushes it through the card heat sink and out the bracket vents. The exhaust passes through a window in the exhaust bulkhead into the central plenum.
5. The crown turbine (3 x ebm-papst R3G310 EC impellers, N+1, on a 56 V bus) holds the plenum below the column interior, so the cards never fight back-pressure. It sends the heat up the 18 in chimney.
6. Host-node air (plinth arms) and the base Peltier hot sides discharge into the hub and join the plenum. None of that heat is released back into the room at intake height.

## Honest reading

- The base Peltier modules are **trim only**: 4 x 110 W of cooling per column against about 1.6 kW of coil duty. Their real job is closing the last 1 to 2 K and holding the coolant above dew point when the chiller cycles. The main chilling is the 17.5 C water loop from a 2-ton-class chiller or building chilled water through a plate heat exchanger.
- The coil surface never goes below dew point + 2 K, so it runs dry. A drip tray is still fitted under each coil for the humid-day case, where CCM-1 raises the coolant set point.
- With one impeller failed, the other two at 100 % give about 99 % of the design plenum flow. The GPU blowers carry the remainder, and the plenum set point relaxes by a few pascals. CCM-1 and TMC-1 raise an alarm but do not throttle.
- The GPU core estimate uses a generic blower-card resistance. NVIDIA's real fan curve and slowdown behaviour replace it at first article.
